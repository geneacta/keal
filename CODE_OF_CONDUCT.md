# Code of Conduct

## Everyone is welcome here

This project is for everyone. You belong in it whatever your gender
identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, appearance, body
size, race, ethnicity, caste, age, religion, nationality, or any other
characteristic of who you are. That is not a disclaimer at the bottom of
the page; it is the first rule, and it outranks every other consideration
in this file.

**And it applies just as much to what you know.** Keal is a compiler
project, which means it is full of words — monomorphisation, refcounting,
unwinding, bootstrapping — that nobody was born knowing. Not knowing one
of them is a completely ordinary state to be in, and asking is exactly
the right thing to do. A question that seems basic is a gift: it usually
means the documentation failed, and that is our bug, not yours.

So, concretely:

* **Answer the question that was asked**, kindly, without a preamble
  about how the asker should have known. "Read the source" is not an
  answer. "Here's the file, and here's the paragraph that explains it"
  is.
* **Nobody is required to prove they are qualified** before taking part.
  There is no experience threshold for opening an issue, asking why
  something works the way it does, or proposing a change.
* **A first patch is a good patch to review generously.** Say what is
  right about it before what is wrong with it, and say what to change
  rather than only that something is wrong.
* **Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith**,
  including when they disagree with you and including when they are
  clearly newer at this than you are.

## Being a good participant

Be kind and courteous. There is no need to be mean or rude, and no
technical point is improved by being made unkindly.

Respect that people differ in judgement. Every design and implementation
choice in this language carries trade-offs and costs — that is why the
`docs/` directory argues with itself in the open. There is seldom one
right answer, and "I would have done it differently" is a conversation,
not a verdict.

Keep unstructured critique to a minimum. If you have an idea you believe
in, fork the repository and find out how it works; a branch that runs is
worth a great deal more than a paragraph about what someone else should
build. Concrete criticism of concrete work is always welcome.

Please avoid overtly sexual aliases or nicknames that would make this a
less comfortable place for other people.

Spamming, trolling, flaming, baiting and other attention-stealing
behaviour are not welcome.

## What we will not accept

We will exclude you from this project if you insult, demean or harass
anyone. We read "harassment" broadly, including the definition given in
the [Citizen Code of Conduct](https://github.com/stumpsyn/policies/blob/master/citizen_code_of_conduct.md);
if you are unsure whether something counts, read that definition. In
particular, behaviour that pushes out people from socially marginalised
groups is not tolerated here, whatever form it takes and however
technically capable the person doing it may be.

Private harassment is equally unacceptable. If someone has made you
uncomfortable — in an issue, a pull request, a review, or in private —
tell us. It does not matter whether you are a regular contributor or
someone who opened their first issue an hour ago. We care about this
being a place you can take part in safely, and we will act.

## Reporting

Write to **contact@geneacta.com**. Reports are read by the maintainers
and treated confidentially. Tell us what happened and what you would
like to see done; you are not required to have a remedy in mind.

We would rather hear about something small and be told it was nothing
than have you leave without saying anything.
